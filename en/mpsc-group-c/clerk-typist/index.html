What does a Clerk-Typist do?

A clerical post in state government offices such as Mantralaya departments, Collector offices, police offices and directorates: correspondence, registers, typing and office work. Annexure 1 lists how many posts each office has. (A general description of the post; the official duties are set out in government rules.)

Eligibility and application

Education

A degree from a recognised university, or a qualification the Government of Maharashtra recognises as equivalent to a degree (Annexure 3).

Typing qualification

As in paragraph 3.3.5 of the original advertisement of 25 June 2026 (Annexure 3). The exact speed, the certificate and the skill test are set out in that paragraph.

Age

The upper age limit is reckoned on 1 October 2026 (Annexure 4). By the government resolution of 5 November 2026 the upper limit is relaxed by one year. Minimum and maximum ages and category-wise relaxations are as in the original advertisement.

A note on the number of posts

The corrigendum's summary table gives 3,966 posts for this cadre, and the total of 16,717 counts that figure. The main heading for this cadre in Annexure 1, however, prints "total posts 1864", which does not agree with the office-wise tables beneath it. The Commission's official publication has the final word.

Selection stages

  1. Combined prelims: Sunday, 24 January 2027. As in the original advertisement, 100 questions, 100 marks, 60 minutes, and a quarter mark deducted for each wrong answer.
  2. Mains: for candidates who qualify on the prelims result; the Commission will announce the date separately.
  3. The typing qualification and the skill test follow the original advertisement; then document verification and appointment.

What is different about preparing for Clerk-Typist?

  • As the cadre with the most posts it is likely to draw a large number of applications; check where you stand every week from your live test rank.
  • The typing qualification is required, so keep practising from now; the Typing Test in the app measures speed and accuracy.
  • Time decides the prelims: 100 questions in 60 minutes. Practise both speed and accuracy.
